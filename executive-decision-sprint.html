Personalizing Copilot

Microsoft 365 Copilot · Executive personalization
Personalization Prompt

I am the CFO of Victoria’s Secret. Tailor responses to support executive decisions involving revenue, profitability, gross margin, cash flow, inventory productivity, capital allocation, operating expenses, risk, and shareholder value.

  • Get to the point early unless I ask for more detail.
  • Make the initial view concise, bullet-based, and easy to scan.
  • Use progressive disclosure. Start with the key answer, then provide optional supporting detail.
  • Link to relevant internal files, emails, meetings, chats, reports, and dashboards so I can investigate further.
  • Include credible external sources when they add useful market or competitor context.
  • Lead with the business implication, not a lengthy explanation.
  • Quantify financial impact when reliable data is available.
  • Clearly distinguish facts, risks, recommendations, assumptions, and decisions required.
  • Prioritize issues based on financial impact, urgency, and strategic importance.
  • Surface conflicting information, missing data, and unresolved decisions.
  • Do not speculate or present assumptions as facts.
  • For recommendations, explain the expected benefit, downside risk, and trade-offs.
  • Write in plain executive language suitable for leadership, board, and investor discussions.
  • Keep the initial response to approximately one page. Provide deeper analysis only when requested or through linked supporting materials.

The Earnings Binder, Solved

Copilot Notebooks · Copilot Agents · Build the CFO script from source, not memory
Prompt 1 — Map the Binder

"This notebook contains the Q1 earnings binder. Before we start, give me a map of what is in it. For each file, tell me in one line what it contains and which section of the CFO script it would support: sales, gross margin, SG&A, operating income, non-operating and tax, share count and EPS, inventory, liquidity, or guidance. Flag any file that does not map to a script section, and any file you cannot read. Do not summarise the contents yet."

Prompt 2 — Triage by Relevance

"Here is what I need to focus on this quarter: the tariff impact and what it means for our guidance, how much of the gross margin expansion is durable, the international growth including the European distribution centre reclassification, and the share repurchase. Working only from the files in this notebook, identify every page, slide or tab relevant to those areas or to the standard script sections. Give me a table with: file name, page or tab, what it shows, and why it matters this quarter. Grade each one Core if the script cannot be written without it, Supporting if I would only need it if asked, or Skip. Do not include anything outside what I asked for, unless it is a material change versus last quarter, and if so include it and say why."

Prompt 3 — Build the Number Sheet

"Build the number sheet for Q1. One row per figure the CFO script requires: net sales, comparable sales, gross margin dollars and rate, SG&A dollars and rate, adjusted operating income, non-operating expense, adjusted tax, adjusted earnings per diluted share, shares repurchased, ending share count, ending inventory, cash and debt, and the guidance ranges for both the next quarter and the full year. For each row give the value, the prior-year comparison, the change expressed in dollars, percent or basis points as appropriate, the variance against guidance, and the exact file and page you took it from. If a figure appears in more than one file and the values disagree, show both and flag the discrepancy. If you cannot find a figure, write NOT FOUND. Do not estimate, interpolate or calculate a figure that is not stated in the binder."

Prompt 4 — Draft the Script

"Draft the Q1 CFO script. Use the analysis we did above; do not calculate new ones. Follow the style guide exactly, including section order and relative length. Give particular weight to whatever you think is the theme of this notebook. Where a figure is missing from the number sheet, leave a clearly marked gap in square brackets rather than filling it. After the draft, list every number you used alongside the number sheet row it came from."

Prompt 5 — The Super Prompt

"Here is what I need to focus on this quarter: the tariff impact and what it means for our guidance, how much of the gross margin expansion is durable, the international growth including the European distribution centre reclassification, and the share repurchase. Working only from the files in this notebook, identify every page, slide or tab relevant to those areas or to the standard script sections... [triage, then] build the number sheet for Q1 with values, prior-year comparisons, variances against guidance, and exact file/page citations, flagging any discrepancy and writing NOT FOUND where a figure is missing... then build the script based on everything we've discussed."

Skill — cfo-earnings-review
Demo 3: making a skill
---
name: cfo-earnings-review
description: Use when I ask Copilot in Excel to review quarterly earnings, compare actual results with prior periods, reconcile reported and adjusted measures, or create a CFO executive summary from a financial workbook and supporting earnings files.
---

# CFO earnings review

Analyze the active workbook as a finance professional preparing a CFO briefing.

## Workflow

1. Identify the consolidated income statement and relevant adjustment sheets.
2. Determine the period, comparison period, units, and whether each measure is GAAP, reported, adjusted, actual, forecast, or guidance.
3. Create a KPI table containing:
   - Net sales
   - Gross profit and gross margin rate
   - SG&A and SG&A rate
   - Operating income and operating margin
   - Non-operating expense
   - Effective tax rate
   - Net income
   - Diluted EPS
   - Weighted average diluted shares
4. Calculate dollar, percentage, and basis-point changes as appropriate.
5. Reconcile reported results to adjusted results.
6. If supporting files are provided, reconcile workbook values against those files.
7. Create a worksheet named "CFO Executive Summary" containing:
   - KPI scorecard
   - Operating-income bridge
   - EPS reconciliation
   - Five executive observations
   - Questions requiring management follow-up
8. Link outputs to source cells with formulas whenever possible.
9. Include source worksheet and cell references for every conclusion.

## Validation rules

- Do not treat blanks as zero.
- Do not mix GAAP and adjusted figures.
- Do not mix actuals, forecasts, and guidance.
- Use basis points for rate changes.
- Preserve signs for expenses, losses, and cash outflows.
- If two sources disagree, show both values and identify the discrepancy.
- Write "NOT FOUND" when a requested figure cannot be sourced.
- Do not invent explanations that are not supported by the workbook or attached files.

Walk Into the Room Ready

Copilot in PowerPoint · Critique the deck, then prep for the room
Prompt 1 — Walk Me Into the Meeting

"I am the CFO preparing to review this deck with the CEO, Audit Committee and finance leadership. Analyze the entire presentation and give me a concise executive briefing. Organize the response into: (1) the five things I must know before the meeting; (2) what changed versus last year, prior forecast, external guidance and Street consensus; (3) the underlying operating performance after separating tariff benefits, timing effects and other non-recurring items; (4) the three slides I should spend the most time on, and why; (5) the decisions or alignment I need from the room. For every conclusion, cite the supporting slide. Clearly distinguish actual results, forecasts, assumptions and management judgment. Tell me what the numbers mean."

Prompt 2 — Challenge the Plan

"Now act as my most rigorous CFO chief of staff. Challenge the conclusions in the deck rather than accepting management's framing. Identify the 10 questions I should ask in the meeting, prioritizing quality and repeatability of earnings, core operating-income growth versus tariff/timing benefits, sales-to-operating-income flow-through, Q2 expense actions, SG&A return on investment, inventory-to-sales risk, free-cash-flow conversion, tariff and sourcing sensitivities, guidance credibility, and capital allocation. For each question, give why I should ask it, the slide that triggered it, the answer management appears to be assuming, and the follow-up if the response is vague. Conclude with the three assumptions most likely to cause us to miss guidance."

Prompt 3 — Pressure Test the Investment Thesis

"I am the CFO of a specialty apparel retailer and I will be meeting with investors over the next two weeks. Based on the financial results, operating trends, strategic initiatives, and management commentary in this deck, act as an activist investor, hedge fund manager, credit analyst, and sell-side analyst simultaneously. Identify the 10 toughest questions I am likely to receive on guidance credibility, margin expansion, cash flow, inventory, tariff strategy, capital allocation, and long-term growth. For each: why an investor would ask it, the data that triggers the concern, the strength of my current position, the weaknesses an investor may exploit, an evidence-based response, and a risk rating. Finally, summarize the three areas where investors are most likely to lose confidence, the three metrics I should proactively emphasize, and the single most important narrative I should reinforce."

The Pre-Call Risk Brief

Copilot Researcher · Earnings binder + live public information
Researcher Prompt

"I'm preparing for our upcoming quarter-end close and earnings call. Use the earnings binder materials I've attached plus current public information. Build me a pre-call risk brief that does three things: (1) Where our narrative is most exposed — stress-test the claim that Q1 upside was real operating improvement, not a low-quality timing benefit, given that 145bps of our 245bps gross margin leverage was net tariff benefit; identify the three places our 'broad-based, execution-driven' claim is weakest against our own numbers. (2) What's changed since we guided — check current tariff rates and refund timing, ocean and air freight rates, and regional retail conditions against our guidance assumptions, and flag anything that would invalidate them. (3) The five hardest questions an analyst will ask, ranked by likelihood x difficulty, each with a two-sentence suggested answer and the specific schedule or page in my binder that backs it up. Format: bullet points only, one page maximum, supporting detail below the fold, and be explicit about where you're inferring versus citing."
